Coming from the terminal

Keep psql. Open Taula for the parts psql is bad at.

Wide result sets, editing a handful of rows by hand, seeing which tables reference which, comparing two databases, and explaining a plan without squinting at indentation. Taula's command bar takes SQL directly, so the muscle memory of "type a statement, see rows" still works, it just lands in a grid.

What you keep from the terminal: certainty about what ran. Taula shows the exact statements before every commit for the same reason.

Coming from a GUI client

Same grid, a different attitude to writes.

The layout will feel familiar: connections on the left, tables in a sidebar, data, structure and query tabs. The difference is what happens when you press save. Edits are reviewed as SQL, journaled with their inverse, and guarded when they look destructive. The assistant and the MCP server are the parts your current client does not have at all.

What you give up: MySQL, Windows, a plugin store, and a long tail of engines. If your day is Postgres or SQLite on a Mac, none of that is a loss.

When not to buy it

Taula is the wrong purchase if…

A refund is easy to get, but easier still is not needing one.

You need MySQL or MariaDB

Not supported, and not promised. See databases for the exact list.

You work on Windows or Linux

Taula is a Mac app, written against macOS 15 APIs. There is no port planned.

You want shared workspaces

No team accounts, no cloud sync, no shared query libraries. Connections are files on your Mac.

Ready when you are.

One payment, every update, no subscription. Download the DMG, add a connection, and you are reading rows in under a minute.
